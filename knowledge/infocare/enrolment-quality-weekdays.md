# Enrolment Quality, weekday patterns and staffing

How the Enrolment Quality % full roll treats quieter days of the week (often Monday and Friday), what the staff roster can and cannot show about them, and how to talk about a centre that runs fewer places on some days. Recorded from a discussion on 10 November 2026. The weekday breakdown and per-weekday capacity described below are ideas, not features of the dashboard yet.

## Does % full roll account for quieter Mondays and Fridays?

No. % full roll is one figure per centre per month. Each open day's placements (children booked in the busier session, morning or afternoon) are averaged over every open day in the month, and that average is divided by one capacity for the whole month: the operating capacity if one is set, otherwise the licence. A quiet Monday and a full Wednesday go into the same average. Capacity does not change by day of the week.

So a Monday and Friday dip does pull the monthly % down, but it cannot be seen. A centre that is 95% full Tuesday to Thursday and 70% full Monday and Friday reads about the same as a centre that is steady at 85% every day.

## Where staff ratios are used in Enrolment Quality

Only in the staffing suggestion for operating capacity, never in the % itself. The suggestion counts teachers on a roster Contact entry at 10am on each open day and takes the most common count in the month. Midweek days usually outnumber the quieter days, so this tends to be the Tuesday to Thursday staffing level. Over-2s are covered at 1:10 (1:15 for sessions of 4 hours or less) and under-2s at 1:5, never more than the licence.

## Small calendar effects on % full roll

- Weekday mix: a month with five Mondays and five Fridays reads slightly lower than a month with four, probably by a point or two.
- Public holidays: a closed day with no bookings is not an open day, so a holiday Monday does not pull the % down.

## Should capacity come from staff ratios?

Staffing follows enrolment. If a centre rosters fewer teachers on Monday because fewer children come, a capacity worked out from Monday's roster shrinks to match. Monday then looks nearly full, and the empty places marketing should be selling disappear from the figure.

There are two different questions:

- How many places could we fill and haven't? Divide by the licence or operating capacity (a fixed figure). This is the marketing question, and the reason the headline % uses a fixed capacity.
- Are we staffing efficiently for the children we have? Divide by staffed capacity for the day (from the roster). This is the operations question.

## Weekday profile idea

Keep the headline % against a fixed capacity, and add a weekday profile: Monday to Friday placements (or %) for each centre, so a dip is visible and can be acted on, for example with a "Monday and Friday places available" campaign for that centre. The daily figures are already worked out from Infocare's daily bookings and roster during each monthly update; today they are averaged away and only the monthly summary is stored. A "staffed for" figure per weekday could sit beside it, so a centre that is both short of children and rostering down on Mondays stands out.

## Evidence that a centre runs fewer places on some days

A per-centre summary can be built from the same Infocare data: for each weekday, the average children booked in the busier session, the average contact staff at 10am, the places those staff cover at ratio, and the % of staffed places filled. Example (illustrative figures):

- Monday: 38 children, 4 staff, 40 places staffed, 95%.
- Tuesday to Thursday: about 57 children, 6 staff, 60 places staffed, about 95%.
- Friday: 40 children, 4 staff, 40 places staffed, 100%.
- Reported % full roll: 83% of a licence of 60. Against staffed places: 96%.

## Is the % better than reported? Deliberate model or reacting to demand

Fewer staff on Monday and Friday fits two different stories, and the roster looks the same in both:

1. Deliberate model: the centre chooses to run a smaller Monday and Friday operation and does not aim to fill those days.
2. Reacting to demand: fewer families book Monday and Friday, so the centre rosters fewer teachers to save wages. The places are still licensed and still empty.

In the second case "better than reported" is a cost-control success, not a fuller roll, and those Monday and Friday places are exactly the gap marketing should fill. Saying "this centre is really at 96%" can hide the very gap the chart exists to show.

Signs that point one way or the other (the data hints, it cannot settle it):

- Staffing tracks the ratio minimum for the children booked, week by week: looks reactive.
- The same reduced roster every week for months, whatever the bookings: looks more like a set model.
- Families asking for Monday or Friday places and being turned away, or waitlist demand for those days: the reduction is a choice. No demand for those days: it is lack of demand.

## How to present a reduced-day centre fairly

- Show both figures side by side, never only the flattering one: for example "83% of licence, 96% of staffed places". The gap between them is the finding: places that are licensed but not staffed.
- The decision that a centre runs a reduced-day model is the user's, not the app's. The natural way to record it is an operating capacity per weekday (for example Monday and Friday at 40), with the roster evidence shown as the suggestion, the same way the monthly operating capacity already works. Without that setting the % stays against the full capacity.
- Use several months of evidence, not one. Count the weeks that fit the pattern (for example "Monday and Friday at 4 staff in 11 of 13 weeks") and leave out weeks with public holidays.

## Limits of roster evidence

- 10am snapshot: a teacher who starts at 10:30 is not counted. A different check time, or the day's peak, could be used.
- Ratios are minimums: many centres staff above them (non-contact time, a head teacher off the floor), so places covered by staff are a ceiling, not the centre's intended capacity.
- Relievers and sickness add day-to-day noise, another reason to look across several months.
- Under-2s change the sums (1:5 rather than 1:10), so the split by age needs to be right for each day.
